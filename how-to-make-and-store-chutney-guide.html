How to Make & Store Chutney

Good chutney needs balance: something sour, something sweet, a little heat and enough salt to tie it together. Here is how to make both kinds and keep them well.

5 sections6 min readReviewed Nov 2026

1

Blend a fresh chutney

For a green chutney, blend a big handful of coriander and mint with green chilli, ginger, lemon juice, salt and a splash of cold water. Add a few peanuts or a spoon of yogurt for body. Taste and adjust: it should be sharp and bright.

2

Simmer a cooked chutney

For a fruit chutney:

  • Toast whole spices in a little oil until they pop.
  • Add chopped fruit, sugar or jaggery, vinegar or tamarind and salt.
  • Simmer gently, stirring often, until a spoon leaves a trail across the pan.
3

Jar it

Wash jars in hot soapy water and dry them in a low oven. Spoon in hot chutney, leaving a little space at the top, and seal at once. Label with the name and the date.

4

How long it keeps

Fresh herb chutneys keep for two to three days in the fridge. A squeeze of lemon helps them stay green. Cooked chutneys with plenty of sugar and acid keep for several weeks in the fridge once opened. Always use a clean spoon.

5

Freezing

Green chutneys freeze well in ice cube trays. Pop out a cube or two when you need them and thaw in the fridge. Cooked chutneys can be frozen in small tubs for up to three months.
