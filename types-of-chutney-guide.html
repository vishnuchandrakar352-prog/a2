Types of Chutney

Chutney simply means a relish that sits beside the main dish. Some are blended fresh and eaten the same day; others are simmered slowly and kept in jars for months.

5 sections6 min readReviewed Nov 2026

1

Fresh chutneys

Fresh chutneys are ground from raw ingredients: herbs, green chilli, ginger, lemon and a little salt. Mint and coriander chutney is the best known. They are bright, sharp and best eaten within a day or two.

2

Cooked chutneys

Cooked chutneys are simmered with sugar and vinegar or tamarind until thick and glossy.

  • Sweet mango made with ripe or green mango, sugar and warm spices.
  • Tamarind and date, dark and sticky, often drizzled over street snacks.
  • Tomato, slow-cooked with onion, garlic and chilli.
3

Nut and coconut chutneys

In south Indian cooking, chutneys are often made from fresh coconut, roasted peanuts or lentils, ground with chilli and finished with a tempering of mustard seeds and curry leaves. They are the classic partner for dosa and idli.

4

Chutney or pickle?

A pickle (achaar) is preserved in oil, salt and spices and is usually much stronger, eaten in tiny amounts. A chutney is softer and fresher in flavour, and you can use more of it on the plate.

5

Chutneys around the world

British-style chutneys, made with apples, onions, vinegar and dried fruit, grew from Indian recipes and are served with cheese and cold meats. The idea is the same: a sweet-sour relish that balances something rich.
